# Policy note: graduate outcomes by field of study

*Based on the Ministry of Higher Education's Graduate Tracer Study, 2020-2025. Method and limits are in the [README](../README.md).*

## What the evidence shows

- **Finding work is not the main difference between fields.** In 2025, between 88% and 96% of labour-force graduates in every field were working. No field is near the 30% employability level the government has named as a trigger for review.
- **Job quality is.** In 2024, 59% of employed Services graduates and 54% of Agriculture graduates were in jobs below skilled level, against 12% in Health. Among employed degree holders, 63% in Education and 58% in Arts & Humanities earned RM2,000 a month or less, against 14% in ICT.
- **Four fields rank worst on most measures:** Services, Agriculture & Veterinary, Education, and Arts & Humanities. Engineering, Health and ICT rank best.
- **Education has been slipping relative to other fields.** It matched the national not-working rate in 2020 (16.3% against 16.4%) and was four points above it in 2025 (11.6% against 7.7%).
- **Nothing in six years of data supports a forecast of decline for any field.** The most accurate forecast is that next year looks like this year.

## What follows

1. **Judge fields on job quality, not only on the employment rate.** An employment-rate threshold will not flag any field. Skill level of the job and starting pay separate the fields far more clearly and are already collected in the tracer survey.
2. **Look inside the four weakest fields before acting on them.** A field average hides its programmes. The Ministry holds programme-level tracer data; the same five indicators can be computed there, and intervention should be decided at that level.
3. **Check Education against teacher-intake plans.** Its low pay and rising not-working rate are consistent with more graduates than teaching posts, but this data cannot confirm the cause.
4. **Treat Business & Law's clerical share as an early warning.** It is the largest field, and 22% of its employed graduates are in clerical work, the occupation group most often named as automatable. Its other indicators are average.

## What would make this analysis decisive

- Programme-level tracer results, published yearly.
- The 2020-2023 restatement on the 2024 definition extended back before 2020, so that longer trends can be read.
- Vacancies classified by field of study, to measure oversupply directly.
- Outcomes one and three years after graduation, not only at graduation.

## Caution on headline figures

The Ministry changed its employment definition in 2024. Comparing the published 2023 and 2024 tables directly overstates the improvement: they put 10.2% of 2023 respondents and 7.5% of the 2024 labour force as not working, which are different bases. On the 2024 definition, restated for 2023, the change was 9.1% to 7.5%.
